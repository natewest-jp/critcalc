## The rules that are easy to get wrong

- ⚠️ **CONTENT-HASHED OUTPUT IS NOT OPTIONAL.** `static/assets/landing` is under `/assets/*`, which `static/_headers` serves `immutable, max-age=31536000`. Re-encoding onto the same filenames ships the new art to **first-time visitors only** — everyone who ever loaded the landing keeps the old plate for a year. New plate → new hash → new URLs. Superseded files are swept. Same contract as `docs/rounds/element-icon-generator.md`.
- ⚠️ **The sky's banding is in the MASTER, not the encoder.** An 8-bit resize of the 16-bit master measures ~78% of sampled rows in a flat luminance plateau _before anything is compressed_; q70→q90 moves that by ~1 point for 2.8× the bytes. **Quality cannot buy it back — dither can.** Don't "fix" banding by raising sky quality.
- ⚠️ **The two regions want opposite things.** FLOOR is where bytes belong (at low q it measured _more_ banded than its own reference — real artifacts on the thin ground lines); SKY is already ~50dB and barely responds. Current: floor avif q90 / webp q94, sky avif q78 / webp q88, `effort: 9` (free, offline). Per-tier AVIF payload ~48/70/106/186KB — comfortably inside a hero budget, easy to dial floor back to q85 for ~30% less.

## Grain (dither over the plates)

- The scene carried a noise tile from the start, but **only on `.sky-bg` (z:0), which the sky image (z:1) covers completely at 21:9 and narrower** — so for a long time it was dithering _behind_ the thing that bands and doing nothing. Grain now lives on `.sky::after` / `.floor::after`.
- ⚠️ **Two different tiles, don't confuse them.** `noise-128.{png,webp}` is near-WHITE (mean 249) and _hazes_ the `.sky-bg` gradient — under `overlay` a near-white blend layer roughly doubles a dark base, which lifts the sky ~25 levels and breaks no plateaus. Dithering needs overlay's identity value, **mid-grey**: `grain-<hash>-128.png`, generated here, seeded, gaussian white noise, **lossless** (a lossy re-encode dithers nothing).
- ⚠️ **Grain is sized in DEVICE pixels** (`--grain-tile-size` ÷ dppx via media queries). Sized in CSS px it was _twice as coarse on retina as on a 1× monitor_ — backwards. At 1:1 it's finer AND dithers better (plateau rows 33.8%→8.2% at 2dppx).
- Knobs: `--sky-grain` / `--floor-grain` — ↻ Nate's values **0.32 / 0.25** since 2026-09-26 (were 0.45 / 0.30). Sky runs hotter — the floor's reflections hide steps.

## Geometry — the horizon seam

- ⚠️ **The sky slice carries 4 extra source rows past the horizon and runs UNDER the floor** (z:3 over z:1). The two boxes used to abut at _exactly_ 0.000px, and an exact meeting is what produces a sub-pixel hairline: two separately-composited layers on a fractional device row, each antialiasing its edge against transparency. Measured 1.7–2.1 levels of dark line before the fix.
- The overlap is **real master content, not a stretched box** — nothing is scaled, no content moves. Amount flows from the manifest so pipeline and layout can't drift.
- ⚠️ **`--sky-h` is the TRUE horizon height and must stay that way.** TwinkleStars/ShootingStars size their host to it and place stars as a _percentage_ of it — folding the overlap into it shifts the whole starfield. Only the sky IMAGE box grows.

## ↻ 2026-09-04 — `npm run audit-scene-plate` measures a re-export

`scripts/audit-scene-plate.mjs` (promoted from five throwaway probes in one evening's plate hunt, Nate-approved) says what a re-export changed against the plate it replaces: HSV sat/val/hue, a luma **quantile transfer**, plateau, clipping, the horizon row, and per-floor-band edge energy. `--against <ref>` (default HEAD), `--tier`, `--json`.

- ⚠️⚠️ **EDGE ENERGY MUST BE READ AGAINST ITS OWN BAND MEAN.** A darker plate has proportionally less absolute contrast everywhere, so raw edge energy falls whenever exposure does and says NOTHING on its own about whether the floor's reflections survived. Two plates in one evening turned on this: a 45% raw drop that was mostly exposure, and a 32% drop at _identical_ band means that was real.
- ⚓ **Two readings, and a crush separates them:** `edge/mean` is the fine star-streak texture, `col peak/med` (column-profile p99 over median) is the BROAD band-reflection column. A crush takes the first and leaves the second — read them together, never one instead of the other. ⚓ And **horizontal vs VERTICAL edge energy localises the change**: the floor's reflections are vertical streaks, so flat vertical energy beside a fallen horizontal one says "same ground, softer reflections" and nothing else.
- ⚠️ **The baseline comes from GIT** — optimize-bg sweeps the superseded tiers as it writes the new ones, so the thing to compare against exists only in history by the time there is something to compare. Hash AND `SCENE_SKY_OVERLAP_SRC` are read from the ref's own manifest: the sky crop's last rows are the FLOOR's art (the seam underlap), so a "sky at the horizon" reading that includes them measures the ground.
- ⚠️ **Encoded tier vs encoded tier, one instrument** — never master vs tier. AVIF smooths low-amplitude detail, and more of it in a darker plate, so that comparison credits the encoder's work to the export.
- ⚠️ **A quantile transfer distinguishes exposure from a curve**: a flat row of ratios is exposure, a rising one (x0.67 in shadows → x0.94 at the max, measured) is a gamma/view-transform change — worth naming, because Nate read one such plate as "basically the original values" while the pixels were 6–33% down.
- 🧹 The plate hunt's four superseded generations were SQUASHED out of history (Nate's call) — each swap is ~1.5MB of binary tiers, so an exploratory round should stay uncommitted or be squashed before pushing.

## Measuring, not eyeballing

Colour/banding claims in this area were all settled by measurement and it's worth repeating: plateau-run counting for banding, per-pixel HSV saturation **plus Hasler–Süsstrunk colourfulness** for saturation (luminance contrast feeds perceived saturation — HSV alone can't see it, and that's exactly what tripped up two of Nate's export rounds).

## Plate re-grade round (2026-09-24) — checks that caught real defects

- ⚠️⚠️ **Export the 8-bit master STRAIGHT from Blender (Dither ON).** A 16-bit RGBA export converted to 8-bit in **Affinity** arrives with NO dither: sky fine-grain σ 0.41 → 0.00, file 17 MB → 7.8 MB, flat runs p99 14px → 200+px (max >1,000px), cloud-line edge 0.405 → 0.083. The file size is the first tell.
- ⚠️ **`audit-scene-plate`'s `plateau` cannot judge banding across a DARKER grade** — a smoother, dimmer gradient raises it. Measure vertical RUN LENGTHS per channel (p99 / max) outside the galaxy region (x 2900–4600, rows < 2200). A red-channel run of 394px was the banding Nate saw; the good masters sit at p99 13–14px in every channel.
- ⚠️ **"clip" in the audit = ANY channel at 255** — on this plate it was always BLUE, in the galaxy band's knots (colour breaks, not white). A levels lift caused it; a curves lift that rolls off (blue max ≤ ~240) fixed it.
- Hue-by-luma bands (sat-weighted circular mean) is what answers "does it shift green in the lights".
- Keep a copy of each master before a swap — `optimize-bg` sweeps the superseded tiers, and the master is overwritten in place, so the previous round is otherwise unrecoverable for comparison.
